## Abstract

**Context.** $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ is one of the most abundant three-carbon oxygen-bearing complex organic molecules (O-COMs) that have been detected in space. The previous detections were made in the gas phase toward star-forming regions that are chemically rich, mostly in protostellar systems. Recently, $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ ice has also been reported as (tentatively) detected toward two low-mass protostars, allowing comparisons in $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ abundances between gas and ice. The detection of $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ ice warrants a more systematic study of its gaseous abundances which is currently lacking.

**Aims.** We aim to measure the gas-phase abundances of $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ in a large sample obtained from the CoCCoA program, and investigate the chemical evolution of $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ from ice to gas in protostellar systems.

**Methods.** We fit the ALMA spectra to determine the column density, excitation temperature, and line width of $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ in 12 high-mass protostars as part of CoCCoA. We also constrained the physical properties of $\mathdefault{C\_{2}H\_{5}CHO}$, $\mathdefault{CH\_{2}CO}$, and $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}CCH}$, which might be chemically linked with $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$. We discuss the possible formation pathways of $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ by making comparisons in its abundances between gas and ice and between observations and simulations.

**Results.** We firmly detect $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$, $\mathdefault{CH\_{2}CO}$, and $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}CCH}$ in the 12 high-mass protostars. The observed gas-phase abundances of $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ are surprisingly high compared to those of two-carbon O-COMs (especially aldehydes). $\mathdefault{C\_{2}H\_{5}CHO}$ is considered as tentatively detected due to lack of unblended lines covered in our data. The derived physical properties suggest that $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$, $\mathdefault{C\_{2}H\_{5}CHO}$, and $\mathdefault{CH\_{2}CO}$ have the same origin from hot cores as other O-COMs, while $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}CCH}$ tends to trace the more extended outflows. The $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}/CH\_{3}OH}$ ratios are higher in the solid phase than in the gas phase by one order of magnitude, which suggests gas-phase reprocessing after sublimation. There are several suggested formation pathways of $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ (in both ice and gas) from $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}CHO}$, $\mathdefault{CH\_{2}CO}$, and $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}CH{=}CH\_{2}}$. The observed ratios between $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ and these three species are rather constant across the sample, and can be well reproduced by astrochemical simulations.

**Conclusions.** On the one hand, the observed high gas-phase abundances of $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ along with $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}OCH\_{3}}$ and $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}OCHO}$ may hint at specific chemical mechanisms that favor the production of ethers, esters, and ketones over alcohols and aldehydes. On the other hand, the overall low gas-phase abundances of aldehydes may result from destruction pathways that are overlooked or underestimated in previous studies. The discussed formation pathways of $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$ from $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}CHO}$, $\mathdefault{CH\_{2}CO}$, and $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}CH{=}CH\_{2}}$ seem plausible from observations and simulations, but more investigations are needed to draw more solid conclusions. We emphasize the importance of studying $\mathdefault{CH\_{3}COCH\_{3}}$, which is an abundant COM that deserves more attention in the future.
