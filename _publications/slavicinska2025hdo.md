## Abstract

$\mathdefault{H\_{2}O}$ is detected in environments representing every stage of star and solar system formation, but its chemical evolution throughout these stages remains poorly constrained. Deuterium ratios offer a means of probing chemical links between $\mathdefault{H\_{2}O}$ in different cosmic regions because of their sensitivity to physicochemical conditions. Here, we present the first detection of the $\mathdefault{4.1\,\mu m}$ HDO ice feature with JWST toward a low-mass protostar, L1527 IRS, which may eventually grow to a Sun-like mass. We measure an ice HDO/$\mathdefault{H\_{2}O}$ ratio of $\mathdefault{4.4\_{-1.7}^{+3.7} \times  10^{-3}}$, where the reported error is dominated by uncertainties in continuum definition and ice band strengths. This fraction is similar to the gas HDO/$\mathdefault{H\_{2}O}$ ratios measured in the warm ($\mathdefault{>100\,K}$) inner cores of other low-mass protostellar envelopes and protoplanetary disks found in comparably isolated star-forming regions. Such a similarity tentatively supports the assumption that $\mathdefault{H\_{2}O}$ vapor detected in these regions is not significantly altered by gas-phase reactions following ice sublimation. It also supports the hypothesis that pre- and protostellar $\mathdefault{H\_{2}O}$ ice is largely inherited in a chemically unaltered state by outer protoplanetary disks. However, the fraction is a factor of ∼4–10 times higher than the gas HDO/$\mathdefault{H\_{2}O}$ ratios measured toward comets and low-mass protostars in clustered star-forming regions. This difference may be due to either gas-phase $\mathdefault{H\_{2}O}$ reprocessing in protostellar envelopes and protoplanetary disks or differences between prestellar conditions of isolated dense cores and the clustered star-forming regions that are more analogous to the environment in which our Sun formed.
