**ICEBEAR** is a JWST Cycle 4 GO program awarded $\mathdefault{44.3\,h}$ of observing time. The project aims to compare the ice and gas reservoirs of interstellar molecules (especially complex organics) in high-mass protostars by combining JWST and ALMA observations. The scientific goal is to investigate the formation and evolution of interstellar molecules and whether they are inherited or reprocessed after being released from grain mantles to hot cores.

## Following molecules from ice to gas

Dust-grain ice mantles preserve a record of chemistry in cold molecular material. As a protostar heats its surroundings, molecules sublimate into the gas, where further reactions can alter their abundances. ICEBEAR investigates how closely the warm gas reflects the molecular inventory stored in the ices and where chemical reprocessing becomes important.

Comparing JWST ice measurements with ALMA gas observations connects these two phases. Chemical models help interpret similarities and differences in the measured molecular abundances, while accounting for the different regions sampled by the two telescopes.

## Reading the chemical and physical history of ices

The project uses ice-band profiles and column densities to explore relationships between molecular species and the environments in which they are embedded. Comparisons with laboratory spectra help constrain ice mixtures and the signatures of heating or irradiation. These measurements connect the molecular inventory with the physical evolution of massive protostellar environments.

## Science beyond the ice–gas comparison

Beyond the COM ice–gas comparison, ICEBEAR's full mid-infrared spectra offer a broader view of the environments in which massive stars form. Molecular and atomic lines can probe warm gas, shocks, and ionization, while their spatial distributions may help distinguish emission close to the protostars from that along nearby jets and outflow cavities within the observed fields. Stronger ice bands provide opportunities to measure abundant ice species, such as $\mathdefault{H\_{2}O}$ and $\mathdefault{CO\_{2}}$, and investigate their thermal processing. Silicate features and the dust continuum can also constrain extinction and dust properties. Together with simultaneous MIRI imaging of the surrounding star-forming regions, these diagnostics can help place the molecular chemistry in its physical context and explore how local conditions influence the observed gas and ice.

## Complementary views with JWST and ALMA

The schematic below, from Chen et al. (2024), links the growth and chemical evolution of dust-grain ice mantles to the regions probed by JWST and ALMA. ALMA traces gas emission from the hot core, while JWST absorption samples ice and gas along the line of sight through the envelope. This complementary view underpins the ice–gas comparisons in ICEBEAR.
